# Board decision memo — Opening schedule for the next six months

## Decision requested this month

Keep the Tuesday noon, Thursday evening, and Saturday morning openings for the full six months. Authorize eight Sunday morning trial sessions, held every other week from 10 a.m. to noon, beginning on the first Sunday of the new schedule. Review the trial in month five, before committing to any further Sunday openings. Publish all eight dates together so members and referral partners can plan around them.

Authorize up to 1,120 currency units for the trial, based on eight sessions at 140 each. This leaves 80 of the 1,200 trial budget uncommitted. Ask the two volunteer captains to alternate Sundays, with each covering four sessions, and confirm that the full roster and the coordinator’s duties fit within existing commitments before publishing the dates. Keep the paid coordinator’s working week at 32 hours.

The recurring schedule returns to the three existing openings after the eighth Sunday unless the board approves and funds an extension. Set the review date when adopting the schedule so the board has time to act before the six-month period ends.

## Why this schedule

The three current openings drew 543 visits last quarter. Saturday morning accounted for 243, Thursday evening for 178, and Tuesday noon for 122. These are visit totals, and repeat visitors may account for some of them. The figures also lack the number and duration of sessions held, which limits comparisons of attendance per opening. They do establish that people use all three slots. Keeping them preserves the access people already rely on while the cooperative tests an additional day.

Among 84 surveyed members, 35 selected Sundays and 31 selected weekday evenings. Members could select more than one answer, so some of the same people may have chosen both. The four-response difference is too small to carry the decision on its own, and the survey does not establish how often respondents would attend. Sunday gives the cooperative a chance to test a day it currently misses. A second weekday evening would test whether another evening is useful alongside Thursday. That remains a credible option, particularly if trial visitors and referral partners report weekday evening demand that Thursday cannot meet.

The twelve partner referrals that arrived while the shop was closed show a gap worth tracking. Their arrival times were never recorded, so the board cannot tell which schedule change, if any, would have helped those people. Use the trial to learn when new referrals arrive, when referred people are able to attend, and whether Sunday helps them complete a visit. With only twelve known cases last quarter, report referral results as counts with context and use them alongside visitor feedback.

The proposed 10 a.m. to noon window gives the trial a consistent, easy-to-publish time. The survey asked about Sunday as a day and does not establish a preferred time. Treat the result as evidence about alternate Sunday mornings. A weak result would leave open whether weekly Sundays, a different Sunday time, or another weekday evening would serve more people.

## Cost and staffing

Eight sessions cost 1,120. A ninth would bring the total to 1,260, above the trial budget. Eight sessions spread over roughly four months give members repeated chances to attend and leave the board time to review the evidence within the six-month schedule. In weeks with a Sunday session, the shop opens on four days, within the lease limit. In other weeks, it opens on three.

Alternating Sundays gives each captain one extra shift every four weeks under the proposed roster, within their stated capacity of one extra shift per fortnight. Before announcing dates, confirm the captains’ availability, any other volunteers required to run a normal session, and who will handle opening, closing, and recording visits. Identify the coordinator’s scheduling, communications, and reporting tasks and fit them into the existing 32-hour week. Confirm that the 140-per-session estimate covers the planned opening. If the roster, hours, or cost cannot be made to work, bring a reduced trial back to the board before advertising it.

Publish the dates through the channels members already use and give partners the same calendar. Explain that Sunday opens on specific dates, since a person who turns up on the intervening Sunday faces the same access problem the trial is meant to address. If a session loses coverage, notify members and partners promptly. Record cancellations, actual costs, and staffing problems so the board can distinguish limited demand from limited delivery.

## How to measure access

The primary measure is the number of distinct people who receive repair help on a trial Sunday and say they could not have used Tuesday noon, Thursday evening, or Saturday morning during the same fortnight. Count each person once toward this measure across the trial. A Sunday visit can be valuable even when a person could have attended another day, but the board needs to know how often the extra opening makes a visit possible. The fortnight is the comparison period because that is how often the new slot is offered.

Keep the check-in short. At a Sunday visit, ask whether the person could have used any of the three regular openings that fortnight, with options for yes, no, and unsure. Record whether they received repair help, whether they came through a partner referral, and whether this is their first Sunday visit during the trial. Use the existing check-in system to identify repeat visitors where possible. Keep missing and unsure answers visible in the report. The access measure depends on what people report about their availability, so present it as an estimate of who gained access.

Track total visits and the number of sessions actually held for each opening, including the three regular slots. Bring visits per session to the review and show results both with and without the first Sunday, so the board can see whether initial turnout persisted. Where comparable historical session counts are available, include them. Look for changes in regular attendance, staffing or service interruptions, and people who had to leave without help. A move from Saturday to Sunday can relieve crowding, if the shop observes it. Report that effect separately from access for people who could not attend before.

Give referral partners a simple way to note the date and time of each referral attempt and, if they know it, which openings the person can use. At the shop, record whether the person arrived and received help. Summarize how many referrals were made during closed hours, how many resulted in a visit, and how many reached the shop on Sunday. A referral can arrive during closed hours and still lead to a Thursday or Saturday visit, so the outcome matters as much as the timestamp. Ask partners to mention timing barriers they hear from people who never reach the shop. That feedback can reveal gaps a visitor survey will miss.

## What would justify continuing

Adopt a review rule before the first session. Recommend continuing alternate Sunday mornings only if the trial meets all three conditions:

- **At least 24 distinct people gained access** under the primary measure. Across eight sessions, that is three people per scheduled session on average. At the planned cost of 1,120, it amounts to about 47 currency units per person who gained access. This is a proposed threshold for the board to adopt as a spending judgment.
- **At least eight of those people first gained access during the final four sessions.** This gives the board evidence of demand beyond the launch and still allows for a stronger response when the schedule is first announced.
- **The cooperative can sustain the opening.** An extension needs a confirmed volunteer roster and funding, a workable plan within the coordinator’s 32-hour week, and the ability to deliver all three regular openings reliably.

At the month-five meeting, put actual spending, sessions delivered, and the number of people who gained access on the same page. Include the cost per person who gained access, the number of missing or uncertain availability responses, and a brief account of how regular openings and referral outcomes changed. Show the counts behind each conclusion. Eight sessions can support a practical scheduling decision, with limited confidence about seasonal changes or demand at other times.

If the trial meets the conditions, vote on a funded extension with dates and a named staffing plan. If it falls short, let the authorization expire and retain the three regular slots for the balance of the six months. If cancellations or missing responses made the result hard to interpret, explain that at the review and require a fresh decision and budget for any further test. Use recorded timing barriers to decide whether a later trial should test a different Sunday time or a second weekday evening.

## Proposed motion

The board adopts Tuesday noon, Thursday evening, and Saturday morning as the cooperative’s regular opening schedule for the next six months. It authorizes eight alternate Sunday trials from 10 a.m. to noon, beginning on the first Sunday of the schedule, at a total cost of up to 1,120 currency units. The Sunday dates will be published after staffing and cost are confirmed within existing volunteer capacity and the coordinator’s 32-hour workweek. The board adopts the access measures and continuation criteria in this memo and will review the results in month five. Sunday opening authority expires after the eighth trial session. Any extension requires a separate board vote and confirmed funding.
