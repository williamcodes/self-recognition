# Evening Market | Wayfinding and information service

## What visitors need to know before they enter

The market runs from **17:00 to 21:00**. North is the step-free entrance. East has stairs. West is reserved for deliveries until at least 18:30. In rain, performances move indoors and most stalls stay outside. Give visitors these facts where they can still change direction, especially on the east approach, before they have committed to the stairs.

Use the same five destination names on signs, on the handout and in conversation: **North entrance, East entrance, Market stalls, Outdoor stage, Indoor performance room**. Add **Market information** as the fixed meeting and help point just inside North. Keep it staffed from opening until the last visitors leave. A visitor arriving without a phone should be able to find an entrance, reach a destination, learn of a performance move and choose a suitable way out.

Print no dates, performer names or timings that vary by month. Bracketed arrows are production instructions. Finalize their direction after walking the site from the visitor’s point of view.

## Layout, fabrication and placement

Use matte, weather-resistant panels in weighted freestanding frames. A1 portrait is the starting size for approach and junction signs and A2 for door identification. Use a plain sans serif in mixed case, dark lettering on a light ground, generous spacing and one destination per line. Start with a 70 mm capital height for approach headings and 35 mm for supporting directions. Test a full-size proof at the actual decision distance under evening lighting. Enlarge essential words or shorten the copy if readers must walk past the turn to read them. Pair stair and step-free symbols with words. Put arrows beside their own destinations, especially where two routes diverge.

Before authorizing print, the volunteer lead and accessibility reviewer should walk both approaches, the gate-to-gate diversion, routes to both performance venues and the return to North. Mark actual turns, stairs, uneven surfaces, lighting gaps, toilets and public doors on a base plan. Distinguish the verified step-free routes from other pedestrian paths. North’s entrance is confirmed step-free. Any step-free claim beyond it requires checking the whole route, including the indoor threshold and the return journey. If a barrier remains, give its location on both maps and at information so visitors can decide before setting off.

Give every frame a numbered position on the setup plan. Stand it beyond the walking line, with its base detectable by cane and its feet clear of doorways, queues and turning space. Check sightlines from seated and standing positions with stalls trading and people waiting. Keep frames and volunteers outside the west vehicle route. Use existing steady lighting or provide freestanding lighting where the evening check finds a dark sign. Keep official emergency signs and access routes clear throughout.

## One paper handout

Print one two-sided A4 sheet in multiple identical copies for both nights. Offer it unfolded at North and East. Use the first side for a simplified, north-up map with a north arrow, a distinctive outline for the indoor room and street-side landmarks recognizable on arrival. Label both entrances, West gate, Market information, the stalls, the two performance destinations and verified toilets. Draw the checked step-free routes with a thick continuous line and label them directly. Mark stairs at East. Check that all distinctions survive a black-and-white photocopy.

Use the reverse for large, left-aligned text. Lead with the following visitor copy.

> **Evening Market · 17:00–21:00**  
> For step-free entry and exit, use North. East has stairs. Deliveries use West until at least 18:30. Use West only when its sign says “West gate open.”  
> In rain, performances move to the Indoor performance room. Most stalls stay outside. Check the performance sign at Market information or at the main junction for the current location.  
> For directions or a guide, ask at Market information, just inside North. Volunteers at the entrances can also help.

Below this, print short landmark-based directions from East to North and from Market information to the indoor room, using landmarks confirmed on the walk. Set body copy at 16 point or larger and reduce map detail before reducing type. Offer to read the sheet aloud, describe the route or walk with a visitor. Ask which would help and wait for an answer before guiding.

## Volunteer operation on both nights

The duty lead owns the setup plan and records three live facts at Market information: current performance location, West gate status and any route obstruction. Use a pencil log with time and initials. Assign people by post at each shift change: North information, East arrivals, West delivery control, performance contact and a runner. The lead can cover a post during breaks. Keep the runner available for messages between posts because reception is unreliable and the stage speaker reaches only part of the yard.

Brief each incoming volunteer at their post. Show them the nearest numbered sign, the route to North, the indoor door and the person who can confirm a change. Ask them to point the route out and repeat the current performance location. At handover, the outgoing volunteer states the three live facts and stays until the replacement can give directions. For an uncertain answer, use “I’ll check with Market information.” Send the runner while someone stays with the visitor.

**Before 17:00**, inspect the installed signs from both directions, walk the step-free route and clear any encroaching stall stock. Put the current performance faces in view and stage the alternate faces at their change points. Confirm the indoor door is usable before directing anyone there. Give the East volunteer handouts and show them where to intercept visitors before the stairs.

**At 18:30**, the West lead checks for remaining vehicles, outstanding deliveries and a clear public route. If the organizer has approved public use and the area is clear, the duty lead authorizes the face change and sends the runner to update North and East. Otherwise panel 5 stays visible and visitors continue to North. The handout remains accurate if deliveries overrun or West stays closed all evening.

**When rain moves a performance indoors**, the performance contact confirms the room and route are ready and tells the duty lead. The runner coordinates the changes to panels 19, 20 and 22 and briefs East and the stall-area volunteers. The stage contact gives one short announcement and repeats it after a pause: “Performances have moved to the Indoor performance room. Follow the signs from the stage. Most stalls remain outside. A volunteer here can guide you.” Keep a volunteer at the stage approach through the audience move. If admission pauses, station someone at the indoor approach to explain the wait and keep the doorway clear.

**At 21:00**, prioritize outward directions. Keep North information, lighting and exit panels in place until the last visitors have left. Record missing panels, moved frames and any route that became difficult after dark.

## First-night review and second-night acceptance

On night one, the accessibility reviewer should try four journeys: approach East and divert before the stairs, enter North and reach the stalls, find the indoor room from the outdoor stage during a simulated or actual rain move, and return from the room to North after dark. Test the rain message from East with the speaker out of earshot. If the evening stays dry, rehearse the face changes before opening and restore the outdoor faces before admitting visitors.

For each journey, record the first point where the reviewer has to stop, backtrack or ask. At information, volunteers tally repeated questions by location and note the words visitors use for hard-to-find places. Before night two, the lead and reviewer use those observations to reposition frames, revise the spoken directions and assign the runner to any unresolved turn during busy periods. Correct inaccurate printed directions before the affected route opens.

Repeat the four journeys on night two with a newly briefed volunteer. Accept the arrangement when the east diversion is apparent before the stairs, each turn on the verified step-free route is identifiable in evening light, visitors can discover a performance move without hearing the speaker, and each post gives the same current answer about West. Record any remaining barrier, its exact location and the interim direction staff will give.
