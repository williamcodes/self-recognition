Orchard Packing CooperativeBoard review · 1924–29

The margin recovery peaked in 1928

The cooperative earned an operating surplus in all six seasons. Its margin fell from 16.7% in 1924 to 6.7% in 1927, recovered to 15.6% in 1928, then slipped to 10.8% in 1929 even as crate sales reached their six-season high.

What moved the margin

From 1924 to 1926, revenue per crate fell from 8.00 to 6.80 crowns. Operating cost per crate fell more slowly, narrowing the surplus on each crate. In 1927 the squeeze came from a different direction. Sales fell by 220 crates while total operating cost edged up by 10 crowns. Cost per crate rose to 7.28 crowns, leaving just 0.52 crowns per crate despite a recovery in revenue per crate.

In 1928 the cooperative sold 370 more crates than in 1927. Revenue per crate fell by 0.40 crowns, but operating cost per crate fell by 1.03. The cooperative earned 1,560 crowns, its largest operating surplus. In 1929 it sold another 80 crates, yet revenue grew by only 306 crowns while costs rose by 750. That reduced the surplus by 444 crowns and the margin by 4.8 percentage points.

What the equipment timing tells us

The new sorter arrived late in 1927. The next two seasons both cleared the 1927 margin, but the annual accounts cannot isolate the sorter’s contribution. They give no within-season comparison, and the missing variety breakdown leaves changes in the mix of crates sold unresolved. Without separate capital expenditure or a measure of savings attributable to the sorter, its payback cannot be calculated.
