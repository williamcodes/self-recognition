Board retrospective · 1924–1929

Orchard Packing Cooperative

Operating margin fell from 16.67% in 1924 to 6.72% in 1927, rebounded to 15.62% in 1928, and slipped to 10.84% in 1929. The rebound followed the late-1927 arrival of new sorting equipment. The accounts establish the timing and the change in recorded costs, while the equipment’s contribution remains unknown.

What changed

1924–26 decline. Revenue per crate fell from 8.00 to 6.80 crowns. Cost per crate also fell, but by less, and the margin narrowed even as crates sold increased.

1927 trough. From 1926, crates fell by 220 and cost per crate rose from 5.93 to 7.28 crowns. Higher revenue per crate only partly offset the cost increase and lower volume.

1928 recovery. Surplus rose by 1,046 crowns. The largest contribution in the surplus bridge came from lower recorded operating cost per crate. Volume rose, while revenue per crate fell.

1929 pullback. Crates reached a six-season high of 1,430. Lower revenue per crate and higher operating cost per crate outweighed the volume gain. Surplus fell by 444 crowns from 1928 and ended 84 crowns below 1924.

What the records support

The pooled 1928–29 margin was 13.19%, or 1.77 percentage points below 1924–26. The post-arrival seasons improved on the 1927 trough, and both remained below the 1924 margin. The pooled comparison places 1927 on its own because the equipment arrived late that year.

The six annual totals provide no comparison that isolates the sorter’s effect. The accounts do not break out varieties, so changes in average revenue per crate cannot be separated into selling-price and variety-mix effects. Changes in operating cost per crate also cannot be assigned to sorting efficiency from these totals. Capital spending is not separately identified, so the records cannot establish the equipment’s full cost, payback, or return on investment.

  • Operating surplus = revenue − operating cost. Operating margin = 100 × surplus ÷ revenue.
  • Volume contribution = change in crates × starting surplus per crate.
  • Revenue contribution = ending crates × (ending revenue per crate − starting revenue per crate).
  • Cost contribution = ending crates × (starting cost per crate − ending cost per crate).

The bridge values the change in volume at starting rates and applies the change in unit rates to ending volume. Contributions reconcile to the observed surplus change before display rounding. They describe changes in the accounts without attributing them to the sorter.
