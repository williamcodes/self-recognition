Top Pocket

Script · 1 / 3

Q1 · First punch. Let it ring for two seconds.

VALThat is a ticket punch. You put it through a ticket. You have just put a hole through Thursday.
KITIt was folded up under the short leg of the bench. Look what it says under my thumb.
VALPlatform Half. Oh, give me that.
KITHalf of what?
VALHalf a minute. That was as long as they could get the mail train to stand. Look at the date on it, Kit. I was twelve.
KITWell, somebody ought to tell the wall.
VALTell the wall what?

Q2 · Masonry slides. Give it the full five seconds.

KITThat it can come back now.
VALThere used to be a blue gate there. My dad had to lift me up to see over it.
KITI can see over it. There’s a little trolley. There’s a sack on it.
VALGet your fingers out of that latch.
KITVal. The label’s got your handwriting.

Hold two beats.

Turn after “handwriting.”

Top Pocket

Script · 2 / 3
VALI know. I used to do the labels. Dad said mine was the only writing the postman could read in the rain.
KITYou sent a whole sack to yourself?
VALRead the little box. That’s the sender.
KITThen where was it going?
VALNowhere. They took the stop off the timetable. Everybody came down anyway. Mrs Bell brought a cake with a train on it. We put all the letters in the sack and Dad shut the gate.
KITWhat happened to the cake?
VALWe ate it. What do you think we did?

Q3 · A bell winds down. Wait for its last tap.

VALOh. That used to mean fifteen seconds.
KITTake this end.
VALYou’ll tear it. Get your arms under it. Under, like a baby.
KITWhat sort of baby have you been carrying?
VALLeave the trolley!

Q2 · Wall moves back. Both haul together on one breath. Let the scrape finish.

KITAll right. You can put the baby down.
Turn as the sack lands.

Top Pocket

Script · 3 / 3
VALThere must be two hundred letters here. Half these houses will be shops.
KITHere’s one we can deliver.
VALThat one hasn’t even got a stamp.
KITIt says “Dad, top pocket.” I think that’s enough of an address.
VALHe made me put it in the sack. Said if I wanted to work the mail I had to trust it.
KITDo you want me to turn round?
VALStay there. May I borrow your top pocket? His coat had the same little flap. Lift it for me.

Four seconds. KIT lifts the flap. VAL slips the envelope into the pocket.

VALThere. That’s one.
KITHow do we do the others?
VALMrs Bell’s girl still cuts my hair. She’ll know who moved where. Turn Thursday over. We’ll need a list.
KITAnd what’s going to hold the bench up?
VALYour foot. Till we finish.

Q1 · Final punch. Hold two seconds, then out.

End.

Top Pocket

Producer’s sheet

Two voices · mono · target 3:00 from first punch to silence. Print the first three pages for each performer.

At the microphones

Val works the station. She can still see exactly where her father’s coat pocket was. Kit, her younger workmate, has been sent to fix a rocking bench. Kit has found a punch and a timetable. Start halfway through their shift, with the mild irritation of two people who like each other.

Keep both voices close. At the gate, have Kit look past Val’s shoulder while staying on mic. After the haul, let both catch their breath through their next lines. At “Stay there,” Kit goes still enough for Val to deliver the letter. Let the small delivery take its four seconds.

Sound cart

Load the three included mono WAVs as one-shots. Start each on the script’s italic cue. Keep the wall behind the breath on its return. The two silent holds need no room effect. All five triggers run in order below.

1. Q1 · punch before the first word. A hard clack and spring return.

2. Q2 · wall after “Tell the wall what?” Five seconds, ending in a stone settling.

3. Q3 · bell after “What do you think we did?” A small mechanical bell runs out of turns.

4. Replay Q2 after “Leave the trolley!” Same movement in reverse story direction.

5. Replay Q1 after “Till we finish.” Kit punches the list. Count two seconds from the strike and close both mics.

Clock rehearsal

Start a stopwatch on Q1. The script has about 390 spoken words. Start around 150 words per minute. Aim to reach “handwriting” near 1:00 and put the sack down near 2:00. Play the cake exchange lightly and pick up the hauling lines on each other’s breath. If the read runs long, tighten the early exchanges so the pocket scene can keep its silence.

For live foley, snap a hole punch for Q1, drag a rough block along a board for Q2 and tap a mug with a spoon in a slowing roll for Q3. Have the producer handle all three.
