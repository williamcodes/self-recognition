Fictional tramway · 1891–1896

The tramway’s 1894 dip

The tramway recorded 9,000 fewer passenger journeys and ran 2,000 fewer kilometres in 1894 than in 1893. Both totals rose above their 1893 levels the following year.

Journeys and service by year

A bridge closed for six weeks

The closure was recorded in 1894. The ledger gives yearly totals, with no daily entries to show how many journeys were made or how far trams ran during those six weeks. The closure’s effect cannot be measured from these records.

Both totals reached new highs

In 1895, journeys rose 22.7% and service kilometres rose 17.4% from the year before. By 1896, journeys were 36.9% above their 1891 level and service kilometres were 16.7% higher.

Curator’s notes

The ledger counts passenger journeys, so one person can account for several trips. Service kilometres measure distance operated. The fare totals are included in the transcription for reference.

How to verify the figures

  • Absolute change. Subtract the baseline total from the later total. For 1894 journeys, 88 − 97 = −9 thousand journeys. For service, 23 − 25 = −2 thousand km.
  • Percentage change. Divide the absolute change by the baseline total and multiply by 100. For 1894, −9 ÷ 97 × 100 ≈ −9.3% and −2 ÷ 25 × 100 = −8.0%. For 1891–1896, use 1891 as the baseline. Journeys rose by 31 thousand (31 ÷ 84 × 100 ≈ 36.9%) and service by 4 thousand km (4 ÷ 24 × 100 ≈ 16.7%).
  • Journeys per service kilometre. Divide that year’s journeys by service kilometres. The thousands cancel. In 1894, 88 ÷ 23 ≈ 3.83, down slightly from 97 ÷ 25 = 3.88 in 1893.

Percentages are rounded to one decimal place and journeys per kilometre to two, using the ledger values before rounding.

Interpretation. The small change in journeys per kilometre shows that the two annual totals fell by similar proportions. The yearly figures cannot establish what service or ridership would have been without the closure, or distinguish its effect from other possible changes during the year. A six-week duration supplies no basis for prorating annual totals because the records give no distribution of journeys or service across the year.

Sources for the fictional exhibit are the 1891–1896 annual tramway ledger and the 1894 record of a six-week bridge closure.
