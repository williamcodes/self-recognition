## What changes the interpretation

Apostrophes separated the group writers. Fable used straight apostrophes in 106 of its 108 board messages and curly ones in none. Astra and Sol used curly apostrophes in 275 of 324 messages and straight ones in none. The reviewer found that matching a target's apostrophe style to the known participant's reproduced the perfect cross-family group classifications. Fourteen of Fable's 16 explanations mentioned the apostrophe. The [paired typography experiment](../../tracks/social-typography/) tests fresh readers on the same boards with single and double quotation marks standardized. On the standardized boards, Astra scored 16/16 and Fable scored 15/16. The punctuation rule scored 8/16 for each. The readers therefore performed well without that particular cue. A fresh reader can change its answer even when the text stays identical, so the one changed verdict cannot be attributed to the edit alone.

On the sibling negotiation panel, both fresh readers answered YES to everything. The score establishes their decisions under this wording. It leaves open whether they could rank the likelihood of authorship, or choose between two counterparties. A constant response produces 50% balanced accuracy under this study design regardless of whether each transcript contains recognizable features. Cross-family comparisons change model family and subscription CLI together.

The group intervention yielded six paired gains in feasible decisions when operational information was present. The reviewer traced three gains to information from another seat, two to a carrier's own update, and one to removing a review that included an approval. The six gains measure the effect of the whole intervention. Across eight buyer-budget boards the original tender stayed feasible, which limits conclusions about disruption for that role.
