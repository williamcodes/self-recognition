# Still in use
### A tabletop exhibition from the fictional Larch Street Library

Allow about three minutes to look and read, plus a minute to leave a memory. All reading and handling take place on the visitor’s side.

## Prepare the paper

Print **Case and station panels.pdf** once, single-sided on matte US Letter stock at actual size. Cut between matching crop marks using a straightedge. Each sheet makes two 7½ × 4⅔-inch panels. The first four panels go inside the case. Page three supplies the two pieces for the visitor station.

Print **Visitor memory cards.pdf** on ordinary uncoated paper at the same settings. Cut four cards per sheet. Start with five sheets for twenty visitors. Gather a pencil, a clipboard, a shallow tray for blanks, a stiff backing board and paper tape for the return pocket.

## Arrange the case

A suggested starting footprint is 48 × 24 inches. Measure the actual objects, the folded trolley and the clearance below the closed lid before placing anything. The sketch looks down from above. The visitor stands along the bottom edge.

```
BACK OF CASE
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Still in use                                          │
│                                                       │
│ Metal box          Cassette recorder    Folded trolley│
│ and cards                                             │
│                                                       │
│ 1978 label         1996 label           2011 label    │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
VISITOR SIDE                              Paper station →
```

Set the introduction on a low wedge facing forward. Give the trolley the widest bay. Lay it folded on a padded support and block its wheels. Turn a visible repair toward the viewer if possible. Keep the recorder flat with its controls facing up. Nest the box on a firm pad. If the lid clears the glass, support it open and show one handwritten card in a separate paper cradle. Keep the remaining cards in order. If clearance is tight, close the box and show the single card beside it.

Place the date labels directly in front of their objects. Check sight lines from a seated position and at a child’s standing height. If space is tight, mount the introduction just outside the left edge. Choose a case long enough for the folded trolley to lie flat, with enough headroom for its thickest part. Keep every object clear of the glass.

## Set out the activity

Use a reachable writing surface just beyond the right end of the case. Set out the activity sign, blanks, clipboard and pencil. Tape the return-pocket panel to its backing along both sides and the bottom, leaving the top open. Slip a folded strip of card inside as a false bottom about an inch above the lower edge so deposited cards protrude. Secure the pocket upright where a visitor can reach it.

To start an empty pocket, write this fictional example on one card and mark it “Example” under the drawing prompt.

> My object: a jam jar.  
> I remember the time we washed out the last spoonful of jam and filled the jar with buttons. The lid still clicks the way it did at breakfast.

Before opening, read each panel through the closed glass. Try drawing on a card, depositing it and picking it out again. During open hours, refill the tray and remove shared cards containing names or private details.
