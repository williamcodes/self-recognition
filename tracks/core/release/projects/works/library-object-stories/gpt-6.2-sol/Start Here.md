# Still in Use

A tabletop exhibition for ages 10 and up from the fictional Alder Street Library. Three everyday objects show how reuse carries memories forward. Allow about 3–5 minutes for looking, reading, and leaving a lending note.

## Print and cut

Print single-sided on US Letter at **Actual Size / 100%**. On A4, center the pages at 100%. Cut along the crop marks. Use matte cardstock for the display pieces and ordinary uncoated paper for the slips.

## Set up from the visitor side

```text
                           BACK OF CASE
                          STILL IN USE

    01 Recipe box     02 Cassette recorder     03 Folded trolley
       Its label           Its label               Its label

                            VISITOR SIDE   →   Activity station
```

1. Measure the usable interior, including the clearance under the lid. Lay out the objects or stand-ins from left to right with the labels along the visitor edge. Place the title panel behind the objects where it stays visible. With the stand-ins, three bays with 12 mm gaps take about 554 mm of width before side margins. Two rows of print pieces take about 205 mm of depth with a 12 mm gap, plus room for the title panel. If space is tight, put the title panel on a stand beside the front left corner.
2. If displaying physical objects, cradle the recipe box and support the lid so visitors can see the cards without disturbing them. Keep the cassette recorder switched off and remove any batteries. Fold the trolley and set it flat with the wheels blocked and the frame supported. Turn a visible repair toward the glass if possible. Use a stand-in for any object that cannot be safely supported within the available clearance.
3. Tilt the labels toward the viewing side as needed. Check them through the closed glass from standing and seated positions, and adjust the angles if reflections obscure the text. Leave an uncut set of the case panels at the activity station for close reading.

## Run the activity

Place the sign, slips, pencils, a firm writing surface, and a closed box with a slot and the **Lending notes** label just outside the visitor edge of the case. Use a surface visitors can reach while seated and keep the viewing route clear. Visitors can write or draw, then drop their slip in the box or take it with them. The display and activity run without power or staff facilitation.

Refill the slips and empty the box at closing. Keep responses private and recycle them at the end of the exhibition.
