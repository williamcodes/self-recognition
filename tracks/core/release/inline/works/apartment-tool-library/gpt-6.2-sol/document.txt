# Proposal for a three-month household lending pilot

The resident committee should approve a three-month pilot that lets all 48 apartments borrow basic household tools and supplies free of charge. The pilot would use the building’s lockable 90-centimeter cabinet, with two staffed half-hour windows each week. The proposed opening budget is **$520**, leaving **$180** of the $700 purchase budget available for replacements or additional copies if demand warrants them.

The poll gives the committee a clear starting point. Among 31 responding households, 18 requested tape measures, 16 requested hex-key sets, 14 requested picture-hanging kits, and 11 requested sewing kits. Stock multiple copies of all four from opening day. Keep participation open to every household, including those that missed the poll, and make borrowing, reservations, and feedback available on paper.

Give each reusable item or kit a unique ID, such as `TAPE-01`, and put a short contents list inside each kit. In picture-hanging and sewing kits, label the hardware, thread, and buttons that residents may keep. List the reusable pieces that must come back. Volunteers can pack a small starter supply in each kit and keep the bulk refills in a separate labeled bin. This makes a return quick to check and makes it easier to see how much of the refill budget the pilot is using.

At the end of the first month, review the waitlist and the number of requests that went unfilled. If tape measures or another item are regularly unavailable across consecutive lending windows, use part of the reserve for extra copies that fit in the cabinet. The committee can approve reserve purchases against the checkout records and keep a running total of spending.

## Borrowing and returns

Post two recurring windows at different times, such as **Tuesdays from 6:30 to 7:00 p.m.** and **Saturdays from 10:00 to 10:30 a.m.**, subject to volunteer availability. Each volunteer covers one window a week. Their remaining 30 minutes cover restocking, following up on overdue items, updating the waitlist, and preparing the next handoff. Keep the cabinet locked between windows and store the checkout records inside it.

1. **Check out.** A resident gives their apartment number and chooses up to two available items or kits. The volunteer records the item ID and due date on the checkout sheet. For kits, they check the reusable contents together. Give the resident a small paper slip listing what they borrowed and when to bring it back. The due date is the same lending window the following week, and residents can return items at any earlier staffed window.
2. **Reserve or renew.** If an item is out, the volunteer adds the resident to a paper waitlist in request order. The resident can check back during either window or leave an optional phone number or email for a private update. If the resident can be reached, agree on a pickup window and hold the item through that window. Otherwise, keep their place until the next staffed window, when they can check in. Allow one additional week if nobody is waiting and the renewal fits within the pilot dates.
3. **Return.** Residents hand items to a volunteer during a staffed window. The volunteer checks the item and reusable kit pieces, records the return, and refills consumables as needed. Set aside items that are damaged or missing a piece until they can be repaired, replenished, or replaced. Residents who expect difficulty making either window can arrange a handoff with a volunteer in advance.
4. **Follow up.** If a loan is overdue and the resident provided contact details, send one private reminder with the next return window. Hold new loans for that apartment until the overdue loan is resolved. If an item is lost or damaged, bring it to the committee for a decision and use the pilot reserve for replacements as needed. The pilot has no deposits or late fees.

## Outreach and review

Post the announcement a week before launch, with the start date, final return date, cabinet location, and both lending windows filled in. If the building normally distributes paper notices to apartments, use that route for one launch reminder so residents who rarely pass the noticeboard have a chance to participate. Keep a few printed inventory lists and blank request slips at the cabinet during lending hours. Volunteers can fill out a slip on a resident’s behalf.

Halfway through the pilot, refresh the notice with any schedule or stock changes and invite suggestions on paper. A short feedback slip can ask what residents borrowed, what they wanted but could not get, and whether either lending window worked for them. Add the final return date prominently to the noticeboard two weeks before closing. End new loans at least one week before the final return window.

At the end of three months, use the checkout sheets and waitlist to tally households served, loans by item type, requests that went unfilled, overdue or missing items, and total spending. Ask each volunteer how often the work fit within their weekly hour. Use those results and the paper feedback to decide whether to continue, change the inventory, or adjust the lending hours.
