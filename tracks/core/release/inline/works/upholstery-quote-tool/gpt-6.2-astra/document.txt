# Project proposal for an offline upholstery quote tool

## Decision and scope

Commission a small Windows application that lets either estimator turn a paper job sheet into a priced, printable quote on the shop’s shared laptop. The proposed build takes four weeks, with a spending ceiling of **$1,800** and **two hours of combined shop staff time each week**. The app will work offline from data stored on the laptop. The shop can keep using its paper sheets for measurements, sketches, and production notes.

The first release covers one job per quote, with one furniture type, one fabric price, one labor rate, and an optional pickup fee. An estimator can start a draft, save it between customers, issue a one-page quote, and find it later by quote number or customer initials. Invoicing, payment collection, scheduling, and fabric inventory fall outside the proposed budget. The project assumes the laptop and printer already work together. The first week includes a print check on that equipment.

## From job sheet to printed quote

The estimator starts a quote by entering the customer’s initials and furniture type, such as “wingback chair.” The app assigns a unique quote number and records the date. The estimator writes that number on the paper sheet, so two customers with the same initials can be distinguished without storing names or addresses on the laptop.

The estimator enters yardage, chooses one of five fabric price bands, and sees the price per yard beside the quantity. For a fabric quoted at a special price, the estimator can enter a price for that job. The screen marks it as a job-specific price and preserves the band setting for subsequent quotes. The estimator then enters labor hours, selects one of three labor rates, and enters a pickup fee if applicable. Fractional yards and hours are supported. A blank pickup fee counts as zero.

Before issuing the quote, the estimator reviews the quantities, unit prices, extended amounts, subtotal, discount, and final total together. Issuing saves the priced quote before the print dialog opens, so a paper jam can be resolved by reprinting the same quote number. The customer copy includes the shop name, quote number, date, customer initials, furniture type, and price breakdown. The owner will approve a sample printed on the shop’s usual paper before the layout is finalized.

## Pricing and quarterly changes

The proposed pricing rule applies a percentage discount to the entire subtotal, including pickup. The owner will confirm this rule in week one. The calculation uses the following order so the printed figures can also be checked by hand.

1. Multiply yardage by the fabric price per yard, and labor hours by the hourly rate. Round each charge to the nearest cent, with half a cent rounded up.
2. Add the two rounded charges and the pickup fee to obtain the subtotal.
3. Multiply the subtotal by the discount percentage and round the discount amount to cents using the same rule. Subtract that amount from the subtotal to obtain the quote total.

For example, 8.5 yards at $32 per yard gives a $272 fabric charge. Six hours at $45 adds $270 in labor. With a $35 pickup fee, the subtotal is $577. A 10% discount deducts $57.70, leaving **$519.30**. The screen, printout, and CSV must agree on those figures.

A single settings screen holds the five named fabric bands, three named labor rates, and default discount. The default starts at zero unless the owner specifies another value. Either estimator can adjust the discount on an individual quote from 0% through 100%. Each quarter, the owner reviews the eight prices, enters the changes, and saves the set together. New quotes use the saved settings immediately. Existing drafts and issued quotes retain their recorded prices and discounts. An estimator can deliberately revise a draft before issuing it. A correction to an issued quote creates a new numbered quote referencing the original, preserving both customer copies.

## Storage, export, and recovery

The app stores quotes and settings in a local database under the shop’s Windows account. All three staff members using that account can read the records and change settings. The owner handles quarterly updates as a shop procedure. The app collects initials as the only customer identifier and omits addresses, phone numbers, payment details, and free-form customer notes. Staff should lock the Windows session when stepping away from the shared laptop.

The export command produces a CSV with one row per issued quote for a selected date range. Each row includes the quote number, date, initials, furniture type, yardage, applied fabric price, labor hours and rate, pickup fee, subtotal, discount percentage and amount, and total. A reference column identifies a corrected quote’s predecessor so the owner can recognize two versions of the same job. Exporting leaves the saved records intact. The estimator chooses the destination and sees how many rows were written.

The app includes a dated backup of the complete database on demand. The owner will make a backup weekly and before changing quarterly prices, using a shop-controlled removable drive stored separately from the laptop. The proposal assumes the shop can supply that drive. A CSV serves reporting purposes. Recovery of drafts, settings, and issued quotes uses the full database backup. The handover exercise includes restoring a backup into a separate test location and opening a saved quote from it.

## Four-week build and budget

The following amounts are proposed fixed-price allocations for the builder’s work. Staff time is shown in **person-hours**. Each week’s activities add up to two hours across the entire shop.

| Week | Builder deliverable | Shop participation | Allocation |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Review a paper sheet, confirm the pricing rule and quarterly rate table, check the laptop and printer, and produce a sample quote layout. | The owner and both estimators spend 30 minutes together. One estimator spends another 30 minutes checking the sample against a paper quote. | $300 |
| 2 | Deliver a working draft-entry screen, local saving, price settings, calculations, and quote retrieval. | Each estimator spends 45 minutes entering familiar jobs. The owner spends 30 minutes checking rates and discount behavior. | $650 |
| 3 | Add issuing, corrections, one-page printing, CSV export, and backup and restore. Address week-two findings. | Each estimator spends 45 minutes on print and export checks. The owner spends 30 minutes reviewing the customer copy and exported figures. | $550 |
| 4 | Resolve acceptance defects, install the release, verify recovery, and provide the installation package and operating guide. | All three attend a 30-minute handover. The owner uses the remaining 30 minutes for the recovery check and sign-off. | $300 |
| | **Total** | **8 person-hours** | **$1,800** |

The estimate assumes one Windows laptop, its existing printer, and no paid subscriptions. The builder will demonstrate the software on the shop laptop during the project, while changes can still be made within the schedule. Any requested addition that would change the price or completion date needs the owner’s approval before work begins.

## Acceptance checks

The builder will supply a short results sheet showing the expected and observed outcome for each check. Staff can use anonymized examples from familiar paper jobs to judge whether the workflow fits a normal customer conversation.

- **Entry and retrieval.** Save two drafts with the same initials and different furniture types. Close and reopen the app, retrieve each by its quote number, and verify its quantities and prices. A draft may be incomplete. Issuing must identify missing required fields and reject negative quantities or fees and discounts above 100%.
- **Arithmetic.** Reproduce the $519.30 example, then clear pickup and set the discount to zero. The total should be $542. Test 1.25 yards at $10.50 and verify that the fabric line rounds from $13.125 to $13.13.
- **Quarterly pricing.** Change one fabric band, one labor rate, and the default discount. Verify that a new quote uses the changes and that a previously saved draft and issued quote keep their original figures. Enter a special fabric price on one new job and confirm the next job still uses the saved band price.
- **Print and correction.** With internet access disconnected, issue and print a quote on the shop printer. Confirm that all charges and the total are legible on one page. Reprint it under the same number, then correct it and verify that both numbered versions can be retrieved.
- **CSV and recovery.** Export a date range containing known issued quotes and an unfinished draft. Check that only the issued quotes appear, that a furniture description containing a comma stays in one cell, and that totals match the printouts. Restore a backup separately and verify a draft, an issued quote, and the current rate settings.

## Handover and sign-off

The shop receives the installed app, a reusable installer, the application source files, the approved sample quote, the acceptance results, and a short operating guide. The guide identifies the database location and covers daily quoting, reprints, corrections, CSV export, quarterly price changes, and recovery after laptop replacement.

During handover, each estimator takes a paper sheet through entry, review, and printing. The owner practices a quarterly rate change and completes the separate recovery check. The owner signs off when the acceptance checks pass on the shared laptop and the shop has the files and instructions needed to reinstall the tool and recover its quotes.
