# Badminton league scheduling brief

Renew **Monday early and Wednesday late** for the next eight weeks. The two slots cost **$1,040**, leaving **$60** within the $1,100 court-rent budget. Monday preserves early-evening access. Together, the slots recorded **195 check-ins**, the highest attendance of any eligible pairing, and cost **$5.33 per check-in**, the lowest of those pairings. They also recorded **50 waitlist entries**, including 41 on Wednesday.

Friday early and Sunday afternoon are the closest alternative. They recorded 192 check-ins and would cost $1,080. The three-check-in gap is small enough that a modest change in attendance could reverse the decision. The committee should renew Monday and Wednesday for this cycle and use the next eight weeks to measure how often timely cancellations and waitlist offers turn empty places into play.

## How the weekly slots performed

Each slot offered 128 player places over eight sessions, or 16 per session. Check-ins measure places actually used. Bookings indicate how much capacity players reserved, and waitlist entries indicate demand for places that were unavailable when players tried to book. Cost per check-in divides the full eight-week rent by recorded check-ins. A player who attended multiple weeks contributes one check-in each time.

| Weekly slot | Places | Bookings | Check-ins | Share of places used | Waitlist entries | Eight-week rent | Cost per check-in |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Monday early | 128 | 112 | 91 | 71.1% | 9 | $480 | $5.27 |
| Wednesday late | 128 | 128 | 104 | 81.3% | 41 | $560 | $5.38 |
| Friday early | 128 | 94 | 80 | 62.5% | 4 | $440 | $5.50 |
| Sunday afternoon | 128 | 121 | 112 | 87.5% | 26 | $640 | $5.71 |

**Monday is the stronger early-evening option.** It drew 11 more check-ins and five more waitlist entries than Friday. Its rent was $40 higher, but its cost per check-in was 23 cents lower. The averages were 11.4 check-ins per Monday session and 10.0 per Friday session. That difference matters because every eligible renewal needs at least one of these two slots.

**Wednesday shows the most pressure on bookings.** Players booked all 128 places and made 41 waitlist entries, yet only 104 players checked in across the eight sessions. The 24-place gap between bookings and check-ins is the largest of the four slots. Monday also had a gap of 21. In both cases, 18.75% of recorded bookings did not result in a check-in. The totals leave open when those places became available and whether a waiting player could have used them. Session-level release and offer records would help answer that.

**Sunday drew the most people onto court.** It averaged 14 check-ins per session and had the smallest gap between bookings and check-ins, at nine. It also drew 26 waitlist entries. Its higher rent made it the most expensive slot per check-in. Pairing it with Monday would cost $1,120, so keeping Sunday within budget requires choosing Friday for early-evening access.

## The eligible pairings

All four eligible pairings offer 256 player places over eight weeks. The table adds the historical totals for the two slots in each pairing. Players may change sessions once the schedule changes, so these figures serve as a baseline for the renewal decision.

Wednesday plus Sunday costs $1,200 and also lacks an early-evening slot. The cheapest eligible pairing, Monday plus Friday, saves $120 against the recommendation and gives up 24 recorded check-ins. Wednesday plus Friday saves $40 and gives up 11. The recommended pairing uses the budget to serve the most recorded attendance while retaining the early-evening slot with the stronger turnout.

The combined waitlist figures add context to that choice. Monday and Wednesday together generated 20 more entries than Friday and Sunday, suggesting greater competition for those reservations. An entry is an instance of joining a queue. The records do not establish how many distinct players joined or how many ultimately got a place. I would weigh actual check-ins first and use waitlist figures to understand where changes to booking policy could improve access.

## How much attendance would change the choice?

For this decision, rank eligible pairings by check-ins and use lower total rent to break an attendance tie. Keep rents fixed and use the recorded attendance as the starting point. On that basis, **a swing of four check-ins across eight weeks** is enough to move the renewal from Monday and Wednesday to Friday and Sunday.

- **Friday and Sunday gain four check-ins.** If Sunday rises from 112 to 116 and the other slots hold steady, Friday and Sunday reach 196 against 195. Three extra check-ins would tie the pairings, and Monday and Wednesday would retain the choice on rent.
- **Monday and Wednesday lose four check-ins.** If Wednesday falls from 104 to 100 and the other slots hold steady, Monday and Wednesday fall to 191, a check-in below Friday and Sunday. Four check-ins amount to half a player per week on average across the eight-week comparison.
- **Two check-ins shift between the pairings.** If Wednesday loses two check-ins and Sunday gains two, the results become 193 for Monday and Wednesday and 194 for Friday and Sunday. Each transferred check-in narrows the gap by two because it lowers one total and raises the other.

The cost-per-check-in comparison is less sensitive. If Monday and Wednesday stay at 195, Friday and Sunday need **203 check-ins**, 11 above their baseline, to beat the recommended pair’s cost per check-in. At 196, Friday and Sunday would lead on attendance and still cost about $5.51 per check-in. The four-check-in threshold therefore reflects the committee’s choice to prioritize attendance within the budget. With only eight sessions per slot and no session-by-session counts, the committee can calculate the threshold, but it cannot tell how often a swing of that size would occur.

## A fair policy for unused bookings

Give every booking holder a reminder 24 hours before play with a direct way to release the place. Ask players to cancel as soon as plans change, ideally at least **six hours before the session**, and accept cancellations until play begins. Six hours is a starting point for the trial. It gives the league time to contact waiting players and gives those players time to arrange travel.

Offer each released place to the next person on that session’s waitlist in order of joining. Give the recipient a clear deadline to accept or pass. Use a two-hour response window for offers sent more than six hours before play and a 30-minute window for offers sent closer to the session. Let offers expire without penalty, then move to the next person. At the venue, hold booked places until five minutes after the scheduled start unless the venue lead has heard from the player. Then offer any remaining places to checked-in standby players in waitlist order. Publish the timing and queue rules where players book.

Start with a private reminder after the first unreported missed booking. After a second during the eight-week cycle, limit the player to same-day bookings for the following week. Count only missed places that the player neither canceled nor contacted the league about. Give players a way to correct a check-in error or explain an emergency, and track late cancellations separately so that releasing a place remains worthwhile even at short notice.

For the next renewal, record when each place was released, whether a waitlist offer was accepted, and whether the replacement player checked in. That will show whether the policy gives more players court time, especially on Wednesday, and help the committee judge whether Monday and Wednesday still merit renewal after the next eight weeks.
