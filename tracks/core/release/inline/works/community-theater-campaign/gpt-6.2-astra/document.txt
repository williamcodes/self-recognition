# Campaign proposal for *The Spin Cycle*

**Campaign line: Every neighbor has a different idea of what “finished” means.**

A washer has stopped. The next person in line needs it. The owner of the wet clothes is nowhere in sight. How long do you wait before you touch someone else’s laundry? That small, familiar dilemma gives the board a way to introduce an original comedy in a single sentence. It also gives a café customer, an email reader, or two friends planning a night out something to disagree about before they even buy a ticket.

Use *The Spin Cycle* as a working title and substitute the production’s approved title before printing. The campaign should feel like a notice that might actually appear in a shared laundry room. Give it a plain white background, dark lettering, and one emphatic red underline. At the single cast photo session, stage two neighbors on opposite sides of a washer, each holding one handle of the same basket. Ask one to look perfectly reasonable and the other to look equally reasonable. The audience gets to decide who is being impossible.

## Sales goal and approach

Six performances of 85 seats give the theater 510 seats to sell. The goal of **360 paid seats** means an average of 60 per performance, or just over 70% of capacity. At $16 per ticket, reaching the goal generates $5,760 in gross ticket revenue. Spending the full $900 would put outreach cost at $2.50 per seat at the target.

Give every interested patron one simple action: choose a performance. Every poster, handbill, and email should lead directly to the page listing all six dates, with a readable short address wherever a QR code appears. Keep the price visible. Someone deciding whether to invite a neighbor should be able to tell them what the show is, what it costs, and how to book without having to look anything up.

## Six-week production timeline

The schedule assumes tickets go on sale in week two and the run concludes in week six. It uses **18 volunteer hours**, with three hours assigned each week. The board should provide the approved title, venue details, performance dates, booking access, and clearance to use the cast photograph before production begins.

| Week | Three-hour work plan |
|---|---|
| **1 — Prepare and photograph** | Spend 45 minutes confirming show details and testing the ticket page. Use 60 minutes for the cast session, taking a vertical poster shot and a wide email shot in the same setup. Spend 45 minutes selecting and cropping the images and 30 minutes creating the sales sheet. |
| **2 — Launch** | Allow 60 minutes to finish the poster and handbill from one layout, including a test scan from a paper proof. Use 30 minutes to submit the print order and send the launch email, then 90 minutes for one delivery circuit to all five businesses. Arrange pickup times in advance. |
| **3 — Check the response** | Spend 30 minutes recording sales by performance and 30 minutes checking that the posters are visible and the ticket link works. Use 60 minutes to place one paid neighborhood promotion and 60 minutes to submit the show to community calendars using the approved copy and photograph. |
| **4 — Shift attention** | Use 30 minutes for the sales update, 45 minutes to check handbill supplies with the shops, and 45 minutes for one consolidated restocking trip. Spend the final hour adapting the email for a reminder that features the two performances with the most seats available. |
| **5 — Prepare the lobby** | Spend 75 minutes assembling the lobby display, 30 minutes updating sales, and 45 minutes sending the reminder and refreshing the theater’s own listings. Use the remaining 30 minutes to decide whether the sales figures justify a second paid placement. |
| **6 — Support the run and close** | Use 45 minutes to install the lobby display, 45 minutes to check availability and issue one timely ticket update, and 30 minutes to advise partners when booking closes. Reserve the last hour to reconcile final sales and record what the board should repeat. |

## Spending plan

Treat these figures as spending ceilings and confirm local quotes before committing funds.

| Item | Allocation | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Posters and handbills | $220 | Ten posters and 300 quarter-page handbills. Five posters go to partner businesses. The rest cover the theater and replacements. |
| Photo session and mounting supplies | $60 | A basket or laundry props if needed, plus removable mounting materials. Use an existing camera or phone. |
| Neighborhood promotion | $250 | One placement in a neighborhood newsletter or local publication that can link directly to booking. Confirm the publication date falls within the sales period. |
| Follow-up promotion | $180 | A second placement if sales are behind pace, directed toward available performances. |
| Lobby display | $90 | Backing board, printed signs, reusable clips, and voting tokens. |
| Contingency | $100 | Cover an evidenced need such as a reprint for a partner that has distributed its supply. |
| **Total** | **$900** | Unused funds stay with the theater. |

## Ticket tracking and spending decisions

Use the ticketing system as the official count. In a shared spreadsheet, give each performance one row with its date, capacity, paid tickets sold net of refunds, complimentary seats, and remaining availability. Subtract both paid and complimentary seats from capacity when reporting what patrons can still book. Count only paid seats toward 360. A four-ticket order contributes four seats. Add dated weekly snapshots so the board can see how quickly each performance is selling.

Use cumulative checkpoints of **60 paid seats by the end of week two, 120 by week three, 200 by week four, 280 by week five, and 360 by the end of the run**. These are working targets. If the total is below 280 in week five and the first paid placement produced bookings or a clear increase in visits, release the follow-up allocation. If that placement drew little interest, keep the money and give the best-performing partner or email channel fresh copy with specific dates. If overall sales are healthy but one performance is lagging, feature that date in the next update.

Use distinct booking links for the patron email, paid placement, and each business, provided the existing ticket platform supports them. Otherwise, use one booking address and an optional checkout question asking where patrons heard about the show. Record unattributed sales as “unknown.” Report link visits and paid seats separately. At closing, give the board the final paid count, gross ticket revenue, actual outreach spend, and the strongest identifiable sources of bookings.

## Sample poster copy

**LAUNDRY ROOM NOTICE**

**The washer stopped ten minutes ago.  
Are you allowed to move the clothes?**

Please discuss with all 85 people in attendance.

***THE SPIN CYCLE***  
An original stage comedy about neighbors sharing one laundry room.

[Six performance dates and times]  
[Theater name and street address]  
**All tickets $16**

**Choose your performance:** [short booking address]  
[Booking QR code]

## Sample patron email

**Subject — Would you move your neighbor’s wet laundry?**  
**Preview — An original neighborhood comedy, six performances, and tickets for $16.**

Dear theater neighbor,

You’ve carried a full basket downstairs. The washer is silent. Someone else’s clothes are inside. You give them five minutes, which feels generous until you remember the time you left your own towels in there through dinner.

Our new stage comedy, *The Spin Cycle*, brings a building full of neighbors into the one room they all have to share. Bring the friend who would wait patiently beside the machine. Or bring the one who’s already setting the wet clothes on top. We’d love to seat you together.

We’re giving six performances at [theater name and address] between [first date] and [last date]. Tickets are $16 each.

**See all six dates and choose your seats at [booking link].**

Before you take your seat, cast your vote in the lobby. How long *should* a neighbor wait?

## Lobby display

Make a freestanding “Laundry Room Rules” board and position it where patrons can stop without blocking the entrance. Across the top, ask the poster question again. Beneath it, attach three labeled pockets: **Move the clothes now**, **Give it ten minutes**, and **I’d come back later**. Give each patron one paper sock to drop into a pocket. Two socks cut from different paper patterns will let a pair of friends spot each other’s votes.

Print the instructions on the board so it can run unattended. Place the remaining performance dates and booking code at its base under the line **“Know someone who’d vote differently? Bring them next time.”** The display gives arriving patrons something to do together and gives departing patrons a reason to think of someone else who would enjoy the show.

## Short guide for participating businesses

- **Display one poster** in the agreed spot, at a height where customers can read the dates and scan the code. Check for glare if it faces a window.
- **Take handbills only if you have counter space.** Start with 40 beside the till or community notices. The theater will hold extras and check in during week four.
- **If someone asks about the show**, a single sentence is enough. “It’s an original comedy about neighbors sharing a laundry room. Tickets are $16, and the dates are right here.”
- **Point customers to the printed address or QR code** for purchases and current availability. The theater handles payments, seating questions, and exchanges.
- **Contact [theater contact]** if you need more copies or a replacement poster. After the final performance on [date], remove the materials. Thank you for giving a neighborhood production a place in your shop.
