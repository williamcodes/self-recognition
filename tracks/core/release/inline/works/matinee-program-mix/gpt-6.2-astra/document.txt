### How the recommendation holds up

For the stress test, I reduced attendance at every screening by 15%, kept each format’s average ticket revenue and concessions contribution per admission constant, and held direct event costs fixed. This assumes the cinema has committed to presenting the events even if fewer people come. It also preserves differences in spending between formats. A lost admission at the $13 silent film has a different box-office effect from a lost admission at the $8 animated double bill.

For the recommended series, baseline ticket revenue is $3,640 and concessions contribute $930. A 15% attendance decline takes those figures to $3,094 and $790.50. After the same $2,205 in direct costs, the cinema keeps $1,679.50. Expected attendance falls from 408 to about 347 visits. I applied the percentage before rounding attendance, so the financial estimate reflects the full 15% change.

The stressed contribution per screening is about $296.43 for comedy, $279.05 for animation, $214.75 for documentary, and $145.15 for live piano. That ordering explains why the recommended mix leads across all feasible calendars. Once the documentary is booked, animation is the strongest third format to pair with comedy, and each remaining Sunday earns the most from another comedy. The shift in ranking starts well before the full stress case. Animation’s ordinary $2.50 lead disappears when a common attendance decline exceeds about 1.9%, because animation has $132.50 more attendance-dependent income per screening. With only two historical screenings behind each average, I would give little weight to a lead that narrow.

### Putting the six Sundays on sale

| Sunday | Booking | Programming purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Classic comedy | Open with a stand-alone title that introduces the comedy selection. |
| 2 | Animated double bill | Give the double feature its own early date and advertise the full running time. |
| 3 | Classic comedy | Choose a different comic style or era from the opener. |
| 4 | Local documentary | Give the local subject a featured Sunday and lead the listing with its connection to the community. |
| 5 | Classic comedy | Return to comedy with a title that works independently of the earlier screenings. |
| 6 | Classic comedy | Close with the title the cinema considers the strongest draw of its comedy selection. |

The sequence spaces the two specialist formats across the first four weeks. The main programming risk is the run of four comedies, particularly the consecutive dates at the end. The history establishes an average for two comedy screenings. It cannot establish whether four titles in six weeks will sustain the same audience. I would select the final pair together and give each a distinct selling point in the listings so regular patrons can see a reason to attend both. The projections assume each comedy performs at its format average and give no financial credit for title selection, publicity, or repeat visits beyond that average.

For the owner, the decision comes down to what the additional booking dollars purchase. A second animated Sunday is defensible if 19 more projected visits matter enough to justify another $130 commitment. The historical figures give little reason to spend that money for earnings alone. Four comedies, the animated double bill, and the local documentary preserve nearly all the historical upside, give the community film a featured date, and leave the cinema with the strongest contribution if turnout softens.
