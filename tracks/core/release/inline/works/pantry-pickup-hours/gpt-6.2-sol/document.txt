# Decision memo | Weekly food pantry pickup

**Decision requested**  
Approve Thursday evening pickup for the next four-week period, with Tuesday midday as the fallback if two four-hour volunteer shifts go uncovered.

Thursday evening completed 101 pickups, reached 78.3% of eligible households on the trial’s counts, and fit within both limits. It used 62 of the 65 available volunteer hours and $285 of the $300 budget. Its volunteer productivity was essentially the same as Tuesday midday’s, although Tuesday cost less per completed pickup. The split schedule reached the largest share of its eligible roster and had the highest booking completion rate, but it exceeded both caps.

If two volunteers each miss a four-hour shift and those hours cannot be covered, the recommendation changes to Tuesday midday. Planning from the full 65-hour allowance, the absences leave 57 usable hours, five short of Thursday’s trial requirement. Tuesday’s 48-hour staffing model fits within that reduced capacity, provided the remaining volunteers can cover the Tuesday shifts.

## Trial results

Each option ran for four weeks. Booking rate is booked slots divided by eligible households. Pickup reach is completed pickups divided by eligible households. Booking completion is completed pickups divided by booked slots. The reach calculation treats the counts as representing distinct households over the period. If households could book or pick up more than once, the ratios measure activity relative to roster size, and unique household reach needs to be verified from household-level records.

Thursday recorded 23 more completed pickups than Tuesday. Its eligible roster was five households larger, and its pickup reach was 15.4 percentage points higher. The difference appeared at both stages of service. Thursday booked a larger share of its eligible roster, and a larger share of its bookings resulted in pickup. It had 15 bookings without a completed pickup, compared with 21 for Tuesday, even though Thursday started with 17 more bookings.

The split schedule recorded another seven completions over Thursday. It had a higher booking rate and a higher booking completion rate, with 12 bookings that did not result in pickup. The counts show an access advantage worth investigating, especially if some households can attend only one of the two windows. The trial totals cannot tell the board which households needed that flexibility or how many would have collected food under either single-window schedule.

Because each period began with a different household roster, the percentage comparisons account for roster size only. The groups may have differed in work schedules, transportation, awareness of the pantry, or prior experience with pickup. Conditions may also have changed between periods. The board can use the observed results to choose a schedule under the current caps, but should treat the size of Thursday’s apparent advantage as uncertain.

## Resource trade-offs and staffing contingency

Tuesday was the least expensive option in total and per pickup. It left 17 volunteer hours and $110 below the caps. Thursday left three hours and $15. The move from Tuesday’s trial to Thursday’s used 14 more volunteer hours and $95 more cash alongside 23 additional recorded pickups. Both schedules delivered about 1.63 completed pickups per volunteer hour. Thursday’s stronger reach came with a larger staffing commitment at roughly the same observed volunteer productivity and a higher cash cost per pickup.

The split schedule used 14 more hours and $45 more than Thursday while completing seven more pickups. It also had the lowest volunteer productivity and the highest cash cost per pickup. Running it at the trial’s staffing and spending levels would exceed the next period’s limits by 11 hours and $30. The board has no trial evidence that it can preserve the split schedule’s reach while making both cuts. Thursday therefore offers the highest observed reach among the schedules that fit the available resources.

For the absence scenario, the three hours of headroom under Thursday’s plan can absorb only part of the eight-hour loss. Even if the pantry can staff all 65 hours before absences, it would have 57 hours afterward. If it secures only the 62 hours needed for the usual Thursday schedule, the same absences leave 54 hours. Tuesday fits under either scenario, with nine or six hours to spare respectively, and its trial cash cost stays within the $300 limit. A four-week hours total can still hide a shortage on a particular shift. Before opening bookings, the coordinator should confirm coverage for each Thursday pickup, including setup and close. If replacement volunteers can cover the missed shifts within both caps, keep Thursday. If coverage remains short, use Tuesday for the period and communicate the schedule before households book.

## Measures for the next four-week trial

1. **Track unique household reach through each stage.** Record the opening eligible roster using a consistent eligibility rule and a household identifier. Count distinct households that receive an invitation, book at least once, and complete at least one pickup. Report completed households as a share of the opening roster and track households added during the period separately. Keep slot totals as well so the board can see repeat use and reconcile the results with the earlier trials.

2. **Record where bookings and pickups fall through.** Track offered slots, filled slots, waitlisted requests, cancellations, reschedules, and missed pickups. Report both the share of booked slots that end in pickup and the share of booking households that collect food at least once. A household that misses its first booking and picks up after rescheduling affects those measures differently. Offer a short, optional question about which pickup windows a household can use, including a way for households that never booked to respond. That will help distinguish a schedule barrier from a shortage of slots or an invitation that never reached the household.

3. **Measure staffing and spending by week.** Log planned and actual volunteer hours for scheduling, setup, distribution, and close. Record missed shifts, replacement coverage, and any work left uncovered. Track cash expenses using the same categories across weeks and schedules. Review cumulative hours and spending weekly against the 65-hour and $300 limits, and report completed pickups per volunteer hour and cash cost per completed pickup. Weekly records will show whether a schedule that fits over four weeks still leaves individual pickups difficult to staff.

4. **Make the next comparison easier to interpret.** Keep the eligibility rules, invitation process, and recordkeeping consistent. Record slot supply and any service interruptions alongside the results. If the board revisits Tuesday or the split schedule in a later trial, compare outcomes for households eligible in both periods as well as for each period’s full roster. At the end of the four weeks, review reach, booking completion, and the reported reasons households could not attend alongside actual staffing and spending. Those results will show whether Thursday can reliably operate within both caps and which access gaps a future schedule trial should address.
