# Decision paper for the museum education committee

**Approve one paper architecture workshop and three family gallery games workshops.** This gives families one opportunity to make something and three opportunities to explore the galleries through play. Using half of each format’s two-event supply spending as the estimate for one workshop, the lineup would cost **$219**, leaving **$121** of the $340 supply budget available. At historical attendance averages, it would provide 192 family workshop visits across 216 offered seats. Those are individual attendances, so a family member who comes twice contributes two visits.

The recommendation gives priority to participation. With four dates and equal staff time per event, the committee’s principal choice is how to use each date. Gallery games reached the most people per event, while paper architecture attracted the most attendees of the two clearly identified making formats. The strongest reason to choose a different lineup would be to give families a second chance to attend a making workshop. That is a meaningful tradeoff, and the historical figures put a price on it.

## What the participation record shows

Each row below combines two events. The registration rate measures how much of the offered capacity families booked. The show-up rate measures how many registrants attended. Attendance per event is the most direct measure of how many people the museum reached with one date on its calendar.

| Format | Seats offered | Registrations | Attendees | Seats booked | Registrants attending | Attendees per event | Supplies per event |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Printmaking | 48 | 46 | 38 | 95.8% | 82.6% | 19 | $112 |
| Story objects | 60 | 47 | 41 | 78.3% | 87.2% | 20.5 | $55 |
| Paper architecture | 48 | 48 | 42 | 100.0% | 87.5% | 21 | $90 |
| Family gallery games | 64 | 54 | 50 | 84.4% | 92.6% | 25 | $43 |

Paper architecture and printmaking offer the cleanest comparison. Both offered 24 seats per event. Paper architecture filled its registration lists and averaged 21 attendees. Printmaking came close to filling its lists and averaged 19 attendees. Choosing paper architecture for the making date would therefore reach two more people at historical averages and save $22 in supplies. Full registration suggests that scheduling the making date at a time families can attend matters. Without a waitlist count, however, the committee cannot tell how many additional people would have booked.

Gallery games illustrate why the percentage of seats booked can give a different impression from the head count. The format left ten places unbooked across two events, yet its larger capacity and strong follow-through produced 50 attendees. Only four of its 54 registrants missed the event. For a committee trying to reach families with a fixed number of dates, the consequential figure is 25 people attending per use of staff time. The format also had the lowest supply spending, at $43 per event, or $1.72 per attendee over the two events.

Story objects merits consideration despite having the lowest booking rate. Its two events drew 41 attendees, just one fewer than paper architecture, and cost $70 less in total. It offers the committee a relatively inexpensive way to broaden the subject matter of the series. Its participation record gives less support for using it in place of a gallery games date, where the historical difference is 4.5 visits per event.

## What families said about returning

| Format | Completed surveys | Survey response among attendees | Respondents likely to return | Likely to return among respondents | Confirmed likely-to-return responses per 100 attendees |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Printmaking | 32 | 84.2% | 25 | 78.1% | 65.8 |
| Story objects | 35 | 85.4% | 24 | 68.6% | 58.5 |
| Paper architecture | 25 | 59.5% | 22 | 88.0% | 52.4 |
| Family gallery games | 40 | 80.0% | 28 | 70.0% | 56.0 |

Paper architecture drew the most favorable responses among families who completed a survey. Twenty-two of its 25 respondents said they were likely to return. Printmaking also received favorable responses, with 25 of 32 respondents likely to return. Gallery games and story objects had similar rates, at 70.0% and 68.6%. With just two events per format, a difference that small offers little basis for choosing between them. Gallery games nevertheless produced the largest *number* of likely-to-return responses, 28, because it reached more people and collected more surveys.

Uneven response matters most to the choice of making workshop. Paper architecture’s headline figure of 88.0% leaves the views of 17 of its 42 attendees unknown. Printmaking missed only six of its 38 attendees. Counting the affirmative responses against everyone who attended changes the ranking. Printmaking obtained a likely-to-return response from 65.8% of all attendees, while paper architecture obtained one from 52.4%. This second measure tells the committee how much favorable intent each format actually documented. An attendee who skipped the survey could still be eager to return.

The missing responses leave room for materially different conclusions. If none of paper architecture’s 17 nonrespondents were likely to return, the share among all attendees would be 52.4%. If all 17 were likely to return, it would be 92.9%. The corresponding range for printmaking is 65.8% to 81.6%. These are the limits allowed by the missing answers. There is no evidence about where within them the true shares fall. The committee can be confident that many respondents enjoyed enough of the experience to express interest in another visit. It has much less reason to conclude that paper architecture generates the strongest intention to return across its whole audience. Actual repeat attendance remains unmeasured for every format.

## The choice across four dates

The following options all include a making workshop and gallery games. The estimates assume each date repeats its format’s historical average for attendance and supplies. Fractional visits reflect averaging across past events.

| Four-workshop lineup | Estimated visits | Estimated supplies | Budget remaining |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| One paper architecture, three gallery games | 96 | $219 | $121 |
| Two paper architecture, two gallery games | 92 | $266 | $74 |
| One paper architecture, one story objects, two gallery games | 91.5 | $231 | $109 |
| One of each format | 85.5 | $300 | $40 |

**The recommended lineup provides 96 estimated visits across 120 offered seats.** The four-date calculation is 21 attendees for paper architecture plus 25 for each of three gallery games dates. This corrects the opening participation estimate, which used the two-event totals instead of the per-event figures. Supply spending is $90 for the making date and $129 for the three gallery dates.

A second paper architecture date is the closest alternative. For an additional $47 in supplies and an estimated four fewer visits, it would offer 24 more making seats and a second date for families who cannot come to the first. If the committee values access to making on two different dates more highly than four additional visits, that option fits comfortably under the cap. A series featuring all four formats would give families the widest choice and would also meet the budget. The historical cost of that breadth is 10.5 fewer visits and $81 more in supplies than the recommended lineup.

The largest uncertainty in the recommendation is demand for a third gallery games date. The record covers only two events, and the combined figures conceal whether turnout held steady or varied substantially between them. A useful threshold is 21 attendees. If the third gallery games date attracts fewer than 21, a second paper architecture date at its historical average would reach more people. The museum could schedule the making workshop early and record requests after it fills. That would give the committee direct evidence about unmet demand when it next considers the balance of gallery and making dates.

For this cycle, approve the $219 lineup on the strength of observed attendance and cost. At all four workshops, offer the same short return-intention question at the same point before departure and record attendance and survey completions separately for each date. That small change would let the next committee see whether the third gallery date sustained participation and whether paper architecture’s favorable survey result holds when more of its attendees answer.
