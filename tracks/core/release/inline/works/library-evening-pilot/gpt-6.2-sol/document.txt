# Funding proposal for a six-month trial of extended library hours

**To the Board of Trustees**

## Decision requested

Authorize up to **$5,000** for Hill Branch to stay open three additional hours on one evening per week for 26 weeks. The trial would add **78 public service hours** and provide two staff members for every extended session. Direct staffing costs are projected at **$3,744**, leaving **$1,256** within the cap for verified additional costs. The authorization would expire at the end of the trial, with any continuation requiring a separate board decision.

Hill Branch is the recommended site because it recorded more visits in the final open hour and more requests for later hours than Market Branch in each of the four months measured. The trial would test how much of that interest carries past the current closing time, how much use occurs in the later part of the extension, and what it costs to deliver the service consistently.

## Comparison of the two branches

Each branch measured 20 evenings per month from September through December. The equal observation periods allow trustees to compare both the visit totals and the average number of visits per measured evening.

| Month | Hill final-hour visits | Hill visits per measured evening | Hill later-hours requests | Market final-hour visits | Market visits per measured evening | Market later-hours requests |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| September | 210 | 10.50 | 22 | 150 | 7.50 | 18 |
| October | 240 | 12.00 | 31 | 165 | 8.25 | 17 |
| November | 225 | 11.25 | 28 | 180 | 9.00 | 20 |
| December | 285 | 14.25 | 39 | 195 | 9.75 | 21 |
| **Total or overall average** | **960** | **12.00** | **120** | **690** | **8.63** | **76** |

Across 80 measured evenings at each site, Hill recorded **270 more final-hour visits**, about 39 percent more than Market, and **44 more requests for later hours**. Both branches ended December above their September visit levels. Market’s final-hour visits rose in each successive month, and its 76 requests give trustees a reason to keep it under consideration for a future trial. Hill has the stronger case for the first placement because it led on both measures throughout the observation period.

Final-hour visits show use close to the current closing time. Requests give a more direct indication that patrons want later access, although a person may have made more than one request. The history also combines evenings across each month, so it cannot establish which weekday would draw the most use. The figures support choosing Hill and provide a reference point for setting a trial target. Actual demand after closing will be measured during the trial.

## Schedule and budget

Before launch, the branch manager should select a regular weekday using any available weekday-level traffic records, existing patron feedback, and the ability to roster two staff consistently. Hill should publish the full schedule and its closing time on the library website, at both branches, and through the library’s usual announcements. A fixed evening gives patrons a schedule they can plan around and makes attendance easier to interpret over the six months.

The extension begins at Hill’s usual closing time and continues for three hours. Patrons should be able to use ordinary evening services, including borrowing and returning materials, picking up holds, using public computers, and studying. Staff should record special events and service interruptions so trustees can interpret unusually busy or quiet evenings.

| Budget item | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Staff cost per evening | 2 staff × 3 hours × $24 | $144 |
| Staff cost for 26 evenings | 26 × $144 | $3,744 |
| Reserve for verified additional costs | $5,000 less $3,744 | $1,256 |
| **Maximum authorization** | | **$5,000** |

The wage estimate covers **156 staff-hours** and works out to **$48 per added public hour**. If all 78 hours are delivered and the full authorization is used, the cost is **$64.10 per added public hour**. Before launch, management should verify whether employer payroll costs, shift premiums, or additional closing costs apply and can be covered within the reserve. Any reserve spending should be recorded against the trial. Management should review actual and committed costs monthly and bring any projected overrun to trustees before making commitments beyond the cap.

## How trustees will evaluate the trial

Adopt the following measures before launch so the final decision can be made against a published standard.

| Measure | Proposed standard | How it will be measured |
|---|---|---|
| Use after the usual closing time | Average at least **12 entries per held evening** over the three added hours | Record entries separately for each added hour and divide total entries by the number of evenings held. |
| Use in the later part of the evening | Average at least **4 entries per held evening** across the second and third added hours combined | Use the hourly counts to see whether attendance continues after the first added hour. |
| Reliability | Deliver at least **24 of 26 scheduled evenings** for the full three hours, with two staff present throughout each session | Record actual opening and closing times, staffing, and the reason for any shortened or canceled session. |
| Cost | Keep total spending within **$5,000** and cost at or below **$16.50 per recorded entry** during added hours | Divide actual trial expenditure by all entries recorded during the extension, including entries on shortened evenings. |

The proposed 12-entry target uses Hill’s historical final-hour average as a practical reference for the full extension. The periods differ in length, so trustees should read it as a minimum use target for the added session. The four-entry target gives the second and third hours a separate test and can help trustees decide whether a shorter extension would serve patrons well. At 12 entries per evening across all 26 evenings, the trial would record 312 entries. The projected wage cost would be **$12 per entry**, and spending the full cap would bring that figure to about **$16.03**. The proposed $16.50 ceiling leaves room for modest variation while keeping the cost test meaningful.

Staff should also record how many patrons are already inside at the usual closing time. That headcount captures one way the extension can help patrons who arrived earlier and need more time. Report it separately from entries, since adding the figures could count the same person twice. A brief optional patron question about what the later hours enabled, such as collecting a hold after work or finishing an assignment, would help trustees understand the value of those visits. Keep the feedback anonymous and report the number of responses alongside the themes.

On trial nights, Hill should continue counting visits in the hour immediately before the usual closing time. Where comparable weekday records are available, management should compare that hour with Hill’s pretrial figures to see whether some traffic moved later. Market can continue counting its regular final-hour visits and later-hours requests if those records are already collected. That context can help trustees spot changes across the library system, while allowing for differences between branches and seasonal patterns.

## Reporting and final decision

At week 13, management should give trustees a midpoint report showing evenings and hours delivered, use by hour, spending to date, and forecast spending at completion. The midpoint review should address operational problems such as missed publicity, gaps in coverage, or inconsistent counting. Cancellations and shortened sessions should stay visible in the reliability results, and the final report should show the number of evenings used in each calculation.

After week 26, trustees should receive the same measures for the full period, with monthly attendance, a breakdown of actual costs, and a summary of patron feedback. If Hill meets the attendance, later-hour use, reliability, and cost standards, trustees should consider authorizing the same schedule with a separate operating budget. If overall use is strong and concentrated in the first added hour, the hourly counts and patron feedback should inform a shorter schedule. If attendance or cost falls short, the trial should expire as scheduled. Trustees can use the Hill results and any additional information about weekday demand at Market to decide whether a separate Market trial merits funding.

Approval would give Hill patrons a predictable evening option for six months and give trustees direct evidence on how much later access the library should fund.
