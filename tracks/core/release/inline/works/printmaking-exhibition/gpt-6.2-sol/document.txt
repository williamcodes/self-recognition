# On the Way  
## A commissioning proposal for a five-week exhibition of prints by local bus riders

### Curatorial premise

Riding the bus is one way of getting to know a place. A rider learns which windows catch the afternoon light, where a familiar shop disappears from view, and how much of a journey happens while waiting. *On the Way* commissions twelve local bus riders to share that knowledge through prints. Each contributor may submit one or two works based on their own experience of riding, waiting for, or transferring between buses.

The invitation to contributors will ask for a particular observation. It might be a gesture between strangers, a view seen every morning, or the way a long wait changes the feeling of a street. Contributors can work in any print process they already use. The exhibition will make room for pleasure, boredom, care, and frustration, wherever those experiences appear in the work. A visitor who rides the same route may recognize a place. Another may recognize a feeling from a completely different journey.

### Commission, selection, and payment

Each of the twelve contributors will receive a **$125 commission** for preparing and lending one exhibition-ready print and reviewing its label. A second submission is optional. Up to four second prints may be selected, with an additional **$50 loan fee** for each. The center will pay the base fees on delivery of the agreed work and the additional fees when the selection is confirmed. Contributors retain ownership and copyright. The agreement will ask separately for permission to reproduce a work in the guide or invitation, and contributors will choose how they are credited.

The submission brief will specify a maximum mounted size of 50 × 60 cm and ask for the title, process, year, dimensions, and a short note about the observation behind each print. Contributors may dictate their note to staff. Staff will flag any mounting or handling issues while there is time to resolve them. The exhibition includes one work from each contributor, for a total of twelve to sixteen prints.

One staff member and two programming committee members will make the selection in a single session. For each contributor, they will look for the print that most clearly conveys a particular observation and consider how it reads alongside the others. The second-print places will go to works that add something the first selection cannot convey on its own, such as a return journey seen after dark or the same stop in a different season. The panel will use a simple wall mock-up to settle the final sequence and record a short rationale for each second-print decision.

### Display and visitor experience

Divide the 14 meters of hanging wall into four sections of about 3.5 meters, with three or four prints in each. Start the sequence with waiting, move through boarding and views in motion, and end with transfers or arrivals. Adjust the sequence to fit the selected work. Four mounts at the maximum width take up two meters, leaving roughly 1.5 meters per section for spacing and text. Put the introductory panel at the start and leave extra room around prints that reward a closer look. Set the midpoint of most works around 145 cm from the floor and test label placement from seated and standing positions before fixing it.

Use the display case for a small account of how a print was made. With a contributor’s agreement, show a plate or block alongside a proof and a brief note about what changed between impressions. If a matrix is unavailable, use two proofs or a proof with a photograph of the working surface. Include the case text and a description of its contents in the large-print booklet.

Place the six-foot table near the entrance with folded guides and a sign pointing visitors to the large-print copies. Keep one end clear for reading, with a chair and room to approach the table. On workshop Saturdays the table becomes the demonstration surface. Move visitor materials to a reachable spot by the entrance for the session and restore the table afterward. Confirm the layout against the room’s circulation and exit routes before installation.

### Editorial and design briefs

**Wall labels.** Give every print a consistent credit line with the contributor’s chosen name, title, process, and year. Follow it with 30–45 words drawn from the contributor’s observation, with their approval. Keep the language close to what they noticed. Name a route or stop when it helps a visitor understand the work and the contributor wants it named. Use high-contrast, left-aligned text in a matte finish. Place each label in a consistent position beside its print and include the same work number on the wall label and in both guide formats.

**Folded visitor guide.** Produce a double-sided, six-panel guide visitors can carry through the room or take home. Put the title, dates, and venue on the cover. Inside, use a short curatorial introduction, a simple wall sequence keyed to the work numbers, and a checklist with titles and credits. Give the workshops their own panel with times, capacity, and booking information. Use the back for opening hours, access information, and the center’s contact details. Proof a folded copy at actual size so the sequence reads naturally and text stays clear of the folds.

**Workshop handout.** Make a one-page sheet that participants can follow at the table and take home. Begin with a prompt to sketch one detail from a remembered bus journey. Use four illustrated steps for drawing into a foam plate, rolling on water-based ink, pulling a proof, and changing one detail for a second impression. Leave space to sketch and note what changed. Use the same handout for both sessions so the facilitator can spend preparation time on materials and instruction.

**Invitation.** Write a short public invitation for the center’s newsletter, noticeboards, and community partners. Lead with the exhibition’s title and the fact that local bus riders made the prints. Put the dates, venue, and opening hours in a block readers can scan, followed by one sentence about what they will encounter and how to book a workshop. Include a direct line about large-print availability. Use one contributor-approved image with a visible credit and supply alt text for digital distribution. Confirm all public details before release.

**Large-print access.** Offer a separate, unfolded booklet with the full text of the visitor guide, introduction, wall labels, and case caption in wall order. Use 20-point body type, generous line spacing, dark text on matte white paper, and page numbers. Keep a reading copy on the table, with take-home copies beside it, and make a text version available by email on request. Set the workshop handout in a large-print version too and put copies out with the standard sheets. Staff will test one printed booklet against the installed sequence and replenish copies during weekly checks.

### Fabrication, installation, and staffing

The schedule allows four staff hours per week across three preparation weeks, five public weeks, and one closeout week. Committee members join the selection session. The print vendor produces the paper materials, two paid technicians install and strike the display, and a paid facilitator delivers each workshop.

| When | Work |
|---|---|
| Three weeks before opening | Confirm contributors, send the commission agreements and specifications, check the hanging system and display case, and book technicians, printing, and facilitators. |
| Two weeks before opening | Receive prints and record their condition. Confirm fees, hold selection, draft labels, and send contributor text and credits for approval. Confirm public details and release the invitation. |
| Opening preparation week | Approve print proofs early in the week. Have mounts, labels, guides, and large-print copies ready before installation. Technicians hang the prints and set the case with staff. Staff check the work order, lighting, labels, and visitor routes before opening. |
| Public weeks one through five | Check the installation, replenish guides, and handle contributor and visitor requests. Run workshops on Saturdays in weeks two and four. |
| Week after closing | Technicians strike the exhibition. Staff check condition, return works and case loans, reconcile spending, and share attendance and workshop participation with the committee. |

Each Saturday workshop will run for 90 minutes with up to eight participants. The facilitator leads the session and handles setup and cleanup. Staff reserve two hours of their weekly allocation for room access, check-in, and resetting the visitor table, leaving two hours for booking and exhibition upkeep.
