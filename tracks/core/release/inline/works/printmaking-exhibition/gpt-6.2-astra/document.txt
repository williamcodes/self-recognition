# Between Stops

*A commissioning proposal for a five-week exhibition of prints by local bus riders*

## The commission

Commission twelve local bus riders to show one print each, with room for up to six companion prints. Pay every contributor $150. Give each rider a guaranteed place on the wall and a say in how their work is described. Open the exhibition for five weeks, with printmaking workshops on the second and fourth Saturdays.

The exhibition asks a question that a rider is particularly well placed to answer. **What can you see from a bus because you have seen it before?** A passenger knows when a shop has changed hands because the old lettering still shows through the paint. Someone who catches the first bus sees a bakery with its lights on and its shutters down. On the journey home, a reflection can put the passenger’s face in a window across the street. A printmaker has ways to register each of these experiences. One color can show through another. A repeated shape can change at its edge. The paper left bare can hold the interval between two things seen.

Invite each contributor to interpret the question freely. An abstract print about waiting belongs here as readily as a street scene. Riders can work from memory, and no one needs to identify a route, stop, home address, or fellow passenger. The exhibition should reward the attention it takes to make an image as much as the familiarity of the place depicted.

## Selection and display

Ask each rider for one or two existing, dry, unframed prints on paper, up to 30 × 40 cm, and a few words about a choice they made in the image. Accept those words by phone, in writing, or with the print at drop-off. A contributor submitting two works identifies the one they most want shown and says whether the two can hang separately. Staff will explain the size limit before intake and check dimensions from a photograph or description so that riders can choose work that fits.

The staff coordinator and a programming committee delegate will review the submissions together. Start with each contributor’s first choice. Select up to six second prints where seeing two works together changes the reading of both. The same shelter printed once in pale ink and once in dense black, for example, might make a viewer reconsider what time of day they had assumed. When pairs make equally strong cases, favor the one that brings a different printmaking approach or riding experience into the exhibition. Record a sentence explaining each pairing decision. Confirm the selection, credit line, payment, and return arrangements individually before printing publicity.

Divide the 14 meters of wall into three loose groups of four riders each. Build the groups after selection, looking for visual relationships such as the treatment of a window edge, the recurrence of a shape, or the use of an unusually low viewpoint. Keep companion works adjacent. At maximum capacity, eighteen frames up to 45 cm wide occupy 8.1 meters. Seventeen gaps averaging 20 cm use another 3.4 meters, leaving 2.5 meters for the opening text, group breaks, and end margins. Test the layout at full scale with paper templates, allowing more space between riders than within pairs. Hang works on a common centerline around 145 cm, checking reflections and label sightlines from standing and seated positions.

Use the display case for a closer look at a print taking shape. Invite a selected rider to lend a working proof if one is readily available. If nobody has one to lend, the workshop facilitator will supply a sample plate, a first impression, and a revised impression. Identify who made them and what changed. Place the six-foot table near the entrance with folded guides and large-print copies within easy reach. On workshop Saturdays, move the guides to one end or to the staffed entrance during the session and keep the approach to the wall clear.

## Four editorial briefs

**Wall labels.** Write for someone already looking at the sheet. Give the work number, contributor’s chosen credit, title, year if known, and print process. Follow with up to 45 words anchored in a visible decision. If a rider mentions wiping ink from a particular window, help the visitor locate that window. Use a direct quotation only with the rider’s approval. Give pairs one shared text and identify both works clearly. Set the body in high-contrast, left-aligned type at 20 points or larger. Send each contributor their final wording and credit for approval.

**Folded visitor guide.** Produce a single double-sided sheet with one fold. Put the title and opening question on the front, a numbered wall diagram and contributor list across the inside, and a short return-looking exercise on the back. Ask visitors to choose a print, look at a second work, then return to the first and find something they missed. Include workshop dates, times, booking details, and where to find the large-print edition. Number the works clockwise from the entrance and match the diagram to the installed wall before the print run.

**Workshop handout.** Make a one-page illustrated sheet that can sit beside an ink tray. Show the sequence for drawing into a foam plate, rolling a thin layer of washable ink, laying down paper, rubbing the back, and peeling the impression. Illustrate the reversal of a simple directional mark. Leave a box for the participant to sketch one change for a second impression. Use short commands and clear drawings that remain legible in black and white. Include cleanup instructions at the bottom so the facilitator can devote the demonstration to pressure and ink coverage.

**Invitation.** Lead with a rider’s-eye detail and the invitation to come see who else noticed it. Identify the twelve local contributors collectively, then give the venue, address, five-week date range, opening hours, admission information, workshop booking contact, and large-print availability. Design one version for a neighborhood noticeboard and a plain-text version suitable for forwarding. Use a contributor’s image only with specific permission and an agreed credit. A type-only invitation can go to press as soon as the center confirms the dates.

## Access and public program

Provide four reusable, lay-flat large-print booklets on the entrance table. Set all visitor text in at least 22-point type with generous line spacing, matte paper, and strong contrast. Include the opening text, every wall label, the case caption, and the guide exercise. Give each rider a fresh page and repeat the large work numbers displayed beside the frames. Include a written sequence through the room for visitors who find a diagram difficult to use. Mark the booklets with a large-print sign and let visitors carry them through the gallery. Keep loose large-print workshop instructions at the table on both Saturdays.

Book one facilitator for two 75-minute sessions. In week two, participants will print a small image from a remembered journey. In week four, they will take two impressions from one plate, changing the inking to suggest a shift in light or weather. Each session stands alone. Book six places per Saturday and work in two groups of three. While one group inks at the table, the other looks for an example on the wall, then the groups switch. Participants may work seated and take their impressions home.

## Fabrication, installation, and staffing

Allow four preparation weeks before opening. Cap coordinator time at four hours in each preparation and exhibition week. The fabrication and installation contractor and workshop facilitator have separate paid assignments.

| Week | Work and responsibility |
|---|---|
| Four weeks before | Coordinator issues briefs, confirms dates and drop-off windows, books contractors, and checks wall fixings, table position, and frame availability. |
| Three weeks before | Coordinator logs submissions and condition, reviews selections with the committee delegate, confirms loans and credits, and authorizes contributor payments. |
| Two weeks before | Coordinator drafts labels and sends them for approval. Contractor orders reusable frames and cuts mounts. Center circulates the invitation. |
| One week before | Contractor mounts work with reversible corners, tests the layout with templates, installs frames and case material, and checks fixings. Coordinator checks credits, glare, numbering, and the route through the room, then releases the guides and booklets for printing. |
| Exhibition weeks one to four | Coordinator checks the room and replenishes guides weekly. On workshop weeks, reserve two of the four staff hours for setup, visitor support, and cleanup. Facilitator prepares and leads both sessions. |
| Exhibition week five | Keep the full display available through the advertised closing time. Contractor takes it down afterward. Coordinator checks condition against intake records and supervises a prearranged collection window. |

## Budget and payment

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Twelve contributor fees at $150 | $1,800 |
| Reusable frames, mounts, reversible corners, and hanging hardware | $450 |
| Contract mounting, installation, and takedown | $400 |
| Workshop facilitator, two sessions at $200 including preparation | $400 |
| Workshop ink, foam plates, paper, and cleanup materials | $150 |
| Labels, invitations, folded guides, handouts, and large-print booklets | $220 |
| Contingency | $180 |
| **Total** | **$3,600** |

The budget assumes the center supplies the stated staff hours, furniture, and ordinary room lighting in kind. The $150 fee covers each rider’s participation and a five-week exhibition loan, whether one or two prints hang. Issue payment within two weeks of selection. Contributors retain ownership and copyright. Record any permission to reproduce an image separately, and give each rider a copy of the loan record with the collection date before the work goes on the wall.
