# Proposal for a three-month household lending pilot

The resident committee should open a small lending cabinet to all 48 households for three months. A resident who needs to measure a doorway, tighten a chair, hang a picture, or mend a hem should be able to borrow a kit for a week. The proposal uses the existing lockable 90-centimeter cabinet, gives residents two chances each week to collect or return an item, and keeps the work within one hour per volunteer per week.

Of the 31 households that answered the poll, 18 requested tape measures, 16 requested hex-key sets, 14 requested picture-hanging kits, and 11 requested sewing kits. Those counts give the committee a starting point for purchasing. They tell us less about how many households will need the same item in the same week. I propose buying all four categories, giving the two most requested items the greatest number of copies, and holding some money back until checkout records show where another copy would help.

## Borrowing and returns

Use two fixed weekly sessions at the cabinet. Wednesday from 6:00 to 6:30 p.m. and Saturday from 10:00 to 10:30 a.m. would give households an evening and a weekend option. Each volunteer covers one session and uses the remaining half hour of their weekly commitment for setup, checks, restocking, and reminders. Confirm both volunteers’ availability before posting the times. In the first and final weeks, use that same administrative time for labeling and the closing count.

1. **Choose an item.** Residents can walk up during either session. Each household may have one item or kit out at a time. The volunteer and borrower check the contents card together, note any existing damage, and agree on a due date one week later. The borrower gets a small paper reminder with the item identifier, due date, and both return windows. Borrowing is free and requires no deposit.

2. **Request an item that is out.** Keep blank request slips beside the noticeboard and provide a sealed envelope with a slot for completed slips. Ask for the unit number, item category, and preferred way to receive a reply. The volunteer checks requests at each session and records them in the order received. When a copy comes back, the volunteer tells the first household and holds it through the next session. A resident can choose a folded paper note delivered to their unit.

3. **Return or renew.** Bring the item to either staffed session on or before the due date. The volunteer checks the kit with the resident, records the return, and replenishes supplies from the cabinet. A borrower may renew once for another week if nobody is waiting and the new due date falls within the pilot. During the final week, offer loans only to residents who can return them by the posted closing session.

4. **Handle delays and damage.** If an item is overdue, send a private reminder with the next two return windows. If a piece is missing, tell the borrower exactly which piece to look for. Record damage and set the item aside until it can be checked or replaced. Ordinary use of the supplied hooks, thread, buttons, and needles is expected. Refer unresolved losses to the committee and keep names and unit numbers off the noticeboard.

## Review and decision

At the end of each month, the volunteers should tally completed loans, requests that had to wait, and refill use by category. Count households reached once each, even if they borrow several times. If repeated requests for the same item carry over from one session to the next, the committee can use the remaining balance for another copy after checking cabinet space and outstanding costs. If residents say the hours prevent them from borrowing, try moving one session while keeping the same weekly time commitment. Post any change a week ahead.

Two weeks before closing, post the final return date and invite a one-sentence paper comment asking what residents would keep or change. After the last session, report households reached out of 48, loans by category, unmet requests, total spending, the condition of the inventory, and actual volunteer time. Use those figures and comments to decide whether to continue, adjust the stock or hours, or close the cabinet. Once outstanding loans are settled, keep the anonymous totals and dispose of the slips containing residents’ contact details.
