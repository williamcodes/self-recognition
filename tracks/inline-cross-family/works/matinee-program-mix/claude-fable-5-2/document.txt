# Sunday Matinee Programming Recommendation

## Recommendation

Program **three classic comedies, two animated double bills and one local documentary**. On historical averages this calendar contributes about **$2,368** across the six Sundays on **$2,335** of direct costs, which leaves $665 of the $3,000 budget unspent. If admissions fall 15% across the board, it still contributes about **$1,662**.

No feasible calendar does meaningfully better. The top four options sit within $8 of each other in the base case, so the choice comes down to resilience and variety rather than headline return.

## What each format earns per screening

Dividing each format's two-screening totals by two gives a per-screening average. Contribution here means ticket revenue plus concessions contribution, minus direct event costs.

| Format | Admissions | Tickets | Concessions | Direct cost | Contribution | Return per $1 of cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classic comedy | 67 | $603.00 | $157.50 | $350 | **$410.50** | $1.17 |
| Animated double bill | 86 | $688.00 | $205.00 | $480 | **$413.00** | $0.86 |
| Local documentary | 54 | $540.00 | $95.00 | $325 | **$310.00** | $0.95 |
| Silent film with live piano | 78 | $1,014.00 | $145.00 | $840 | **$319.00** | $0.38 |

Three things stand out:

- **Comedy and animation are effectively tied.** They are $2.50 apart per screening, far less than the noise in a two-screening sample. Comedy gets there on $130 less cost.
- **The silent film takes the most money and keeps the least of it.** It has the highest ticket price ($13 average) and the highest gross, but the $840 cost absorbs most of that. It contributes about $90 less than a comedy while putting more than twice the money at risk.
- **The documentary is the weakest draw but cheap to run.** One documentary is required, and at $310 it earns nearly as much as the silent film.

No format averaged above the 100-seat capacity, so every average is usable as it stands. Capacity does limit upside: the animated bill averages 86, leaving only 14 seats of headroom on a good day, against 33 for comedy.

## Which calendars are feasible

The rules are six screenings, at least three formats, at least one documentary and no more than $3,000 in direct costs. They have one consequence that is not obvious at first glance: **two silent screenings can never fit.** Two silents cost $1,680. The cheapest legal way to fill the other four slots (one documentary and three comedies) costs $1,375, for a total of $3,055. Every other three-format combination with two silents also exceeds the budget. So the real choice is between calendars with one silent film and calendars with none.

## Comparing the calendars

The stress case assumes admissions fall 15% for every screening, ticket revenue and concessions fall in proportion, and direct costs stay fixed.

| Calendar | Comedy | Animated | Doc | Silent | Direct cost | Base contribution | Stressed contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1 | 4 | 1 | 0 | $2,595 | $2,372.50 | $1,627.38 |
| B | 2 | 3 | 1 | 0 | $2,465 | $2,370.00 | $1,644.75 |
| **C (recommended)** | **3** | **2** | **1** | **0** | **$2,335** | **$2,367.50** | **$1,662.13** |
| D | 4 | 1 | 1 | 0 | $2,205 | $2,365.00 | $1,679.50 |
| E | 2 | 2 | 2 | 0 | $2,310 | $2,267.00 | $1,580.45 |
| F | 3 | 1 | 1 | 1 | $2,695 | $2,273.50 | $1,528.23 |
| G | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | $2,825 | $2,276.00 | $1,510.85 |

Calendars A to D differ by $7.50 in the base case, which is a tie. The stress test separates them: each animated bill swapped for a comedy adds about $17 of protection, because comedy carries less fixed cost. Calendars with a second documentary or a silent film give up roughly $90 to $100 in the base case and more under stress.

## How each format handles a bad day

Because costs are fixed, a 15% fall in attendance reduces contribution by more than 15%.

| Format | Base contribution | After a 15% drop | Admissions needed to break even | Fall from average it can absorb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classic comedy | $410.50 | $296.43 | about 31 | about 54% |
| Animated double bill | $413.00 | $279.05 | about 47 | about 45% |
| Local documentary | $310.00 | $214.75 | about 28 | about 48% |
| Silent film with live piano | $319.00 | $145.15 | about 57 | about 27% |

The silent film loses more than half its contribution in the stress case and starts losing money once attendance falls about a quarter below its average. The other three formats can lose close to half their audience before a screening loses money.

For the recommended calendar, the stress case looks like this:

| | Base | 15% drop |
|---|---|---|
| Paid admissions | 427 | about 363 |
| Ticket revenue | $3,725.00 | $3,166.25 |
| Concessions contribution | $977.50 | $830.88 |
| Direct costs | $2,335.00 | $2,335.00 |
| **Contribution** | **$2,367.50** | **$1,662.13** |

A 15% fall in attendance cuts contribution by about 30%, but the series stays comfortably positive.

## Why Calendar C and not the others

**Why not D, which is slightly stronger under stress?** D leads C by $17 in the stress case and trails it by $2.50 in the base case. Neither gap is large enough to act on, since each format's average rests on two screenings. D asks the same comedy audience to turn out four Sundays out of six, and the averages cannot tell us whether the fourth comedy draws 67 people or 45. C spreads the series across two strong formats that draw different audiences, so one misjudged format does less damage. If you would rather keep the extra $130 in reserve and are confident in the comedy audience, D is a reasonable alternative.

**Why not A or B, which top the base case?** Their $2.50 to $5 lead is not meaningful, and they cost $130 to $260 more and hold up worse under stress. The animated bill is also nearest to capacity, so it has the least room to beat its average.

**Why no silent film?** It is the costliest slot and the most fragile one. Swapping one comedy for the silent film (Calendar G) costs about $92 in expected contribution and about $151 in the stress case, and it raises direct costs by $490. That may still be worth doing for reasons these numbers do not capture, such as press attention, the higher ticket price, or goodwill with patrons and musicians. If you want it, G is the version to run, treating the roughly $92 as the cost of a prestige event rather than expecting it to pay its way.

**Why only one documentary?** A second one (Calendar E) costs about $100 in contribution against C. One screening meets the requirement and keeps the local-filmmaker relationship alive.

## Caveats

- **The sample is thin.** Two screenings per format cannot distinguish a $2.50 difference, and barely support a $90 one. Treat the rankings among comedy and animation as a tie, and the gap to the silent film as likely but unproven.
- **Repetition is untested.** The history shows two screenings per format, and this plan runs comedy three times. Alternating formats across the six Sundays, rather than running comedies back to back, should limit audience fatigue.
- **The unspent budget is a cushion.** The plan leaves $665 unspent, and I would keep it as a buffer rather than upgrade a slot.
- **The series will improve the data.** Recording admissions, ticket revenue, concessions and costs for each Sunday will bring comedy to five data points and animation to four, which gives the next programming decision much firmer ground.
